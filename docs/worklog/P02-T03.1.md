## Implemented behavior

Before: every exported `SlotRule` had an empty category list and the blocking gap `gap/p02-t02/slot-accepted-base-categories`. The slot check and hand check answered `unknown`; pools placed only owned items in their source slots, so real imported gear stayed unplaced.

After: the exporter asks the engine. A new `slotAcceptance` rule source calls `ItemsTab:IsItemValidForSlot` for one probe item per base (1756) at normal, unique and relic rarity, against all 113 slots, and against both off-hand slots per main-hand base and keystone flag. Slot rules carry the categories the engine accepts for every base of the category, `refused_rarities` where a slot refuses unique or relic items, and `weapon_sets.off_hand_rules` per main-hand category and flag. Legality decides slot, rarity and off-hand cases; pools place real items by category and rarity. Anything a category or rarity list cannot express becomes a gap rather than a widened rule.

## Adverse cases

Exercised: a ring in the amulet slot (violation); a shield next to a quarterstaff (violation); a one-hand mace next to a two-hand mace (unknown, needs Giant's Blood); a unique jewel in a refusing tree socket (violation) and in a plain socket (legal); a jewel socket on an item (unknown, cites `socketed-item-jewel-slots`, not the P02-T02 gap); a category accepted for only some bases (empty rule plus blocking gap); a main-hand category whose bases disagree (no rule plus gap); a rarity refused for only some categories (no refusal plus gap); a profile without the probe (old empty rules and gap); `owned_from_build` without an origin (TypeError) or with an unknown one (ValueError).

Found by the engine cross-check: the first, categories-only version accepted a unique jewel in seven tree sockets the engine refuses. The rarity probe was added for that.

Untested or not modelled: the parent-item jewel-socket condition (`canSocketJewelBase`); the build's keystone flag state (pairings that need one stay `unknown`); jewel sockets of tree versions other than the default one (the probe runs on a blank build of the default tree; the cross-check skips any socket the probe did not see).

## Deviations and unresolved questions

- Base category changes from `type` to `type/subType` when a subtype exists. Needed because the engine splits flasks by subtype. One synthetic pool test changed its category string to match (`Two Handed Axe/Axe`).
- `owned_from_build` now requires `origin`. The only other caller is the historical review probe in `docs/evidence/PR-14-17/`, which runs against the old reviewed heads.
- Two optional record fields and one record kind are added (`contracts.md`). Old profiles decode unchanged; their meaning (no rule) is kept.
- The real pool leaves four armour/jewelry slots empty because the P05-T02 importer marks 16 of 46 real items `partial` (unresolved lines such as `+4 to Level of all Melee Skills`, `40% increased Energy Shield`, `+9 to Stun Threshold`, Runic Ward lines and `Unique ID:` lines). That is modifier resolution in P04/P05, not slot rules; per-item issues are in `real-pool.json`.
- The committed P02 row slice was not regenerated, so its replayed profile still carries the P02-T02 gap. The P02-T03.1 capture is used instead where engine rules are needed offline.
