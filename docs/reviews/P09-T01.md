## R1 — P2: Separate whole-horizon window totals from elapsed completion time

Locations: `benchmarks/p09-synthetic-v1/protocol.md:89`, `:96`, `:107`; `tests/unit/test_p09_t01_scenarios.py`, `test_every_limit_leaves_headroom_over_its_own_floor`.

The protocol calls the sustained boss's full 30 seconds of invulnerability a floor and says boss completion includes every window. But its first window starts at 60 seconds. A hypothetical constant 2,000 damage/second kills its 40,000-health target at 20 seconds, with **zero** invulnerability elapsed. At 400 damage/second it dies at 115 seconds after one 15-second window; at 250 it dies at 190 after both. The full-horizon 30/270 totals are correct as workload metadata, not unconditional per-evaluation elapsed-time requirements.

The claim that any timeout means evaluator failure is also incorrect: a hypothetical one-damage/second build cannot kill that target within 300 seconds, even with a correct evaluator. The shared scenarios contract explicitly permits valid failed evaluations, including zero-output timeouts.

Repair: describe total windows over the configured horizon separately from windows intersecting `[start, completion]`. Stop the completion clock at death. Restrict any headroom assertion to a fully specified synthetic action model/reference, or remove it. Preserve valid timeout outcomes as evaluation failures distinct from operational errors. Rename/rewrite the misleading test; it currently proves only a numerical inequality between constants.

Acceptance: hand-calculated early-kill, one-window, two-window and zero/low-output timeout examples appear in tests or the repeatable protocol, with the values above. A future evaluator must not add future windows to a completed kill. No actual skill simulation is required to correct these instructions now.

## R2 — P2: Do not claim collection artifacts automatically close the gaps

Locations: `docs/decisions/D04-encounter-suite.md:99`, `:112`, `:116`; corresponding collection-method prose in `scripts/p09_t01_suite.py` (enemy-health gap).

D04 says a build export closes the reference-build gap and a set of recordings closes the remaining eleven gaps. Its own reference gap requires an accepted complete configuration **and** observed completion time. A plain export has no such observation. A visible monster health bar likewise does not, by itself, establish absolute maximum life, all defenses, or the scale conversion between spatial units. A boss/map recording can support some measurements without satisfying every prerequisite. The statement that nobody except the owner can supply the inputs also excludes legitimate separately sourced/exported data without evidence.

Repair: replace blanket closure claims with a checklist per gap: artifact, extractable quantity/unit, required context, uncertainty and reviewer acceptance criterion. A supplied build begins reference selection; it does not freeze an accepted reference result. Timings/bars/positions should close only what they actually measure. Absolute life/defenses require identified catalog evidence or a controlled identifiable experiment; unknowns remain open. Allow permitted independently supplied evidence and the supplemental explicit-link path. Keep owner gameplay effort a concrete optional collection path, not an assertion that all progress requires Elias personally recording it.

Acceptance: apply the checklist to (a) a build export with no trial, (b) a recording with a fractional health bar but no absolute-life evidence, and (c) boss timing observations with no spatial conversion. None may close unsupported fields. One small dataset must not be reported as satisfying the four-scenario, multi-family observation target.

### R1

The oracle no longer presents a horizon total as a per-evaluation floor. The
two fields are renamed to say what they are: `horizon_targetable_time_s` and
`horizon_untargetable_time_s` partition `duration_limit_s`, and the derivation
note states that they are workload metadata, that an evaluation's clock runs
from scenario start to completion and stops there, and that only the windows
intersecting that interval are part of it.

`protocol.md` carries the same correction in all three places the review names.
The timeout table's "Oracle floor it must clear" column is gone, replaced by
the declared route and untargetable time over the horizon with the reason they
are not a lower bound. The claim that a timeout means the evaluator failed is
replaced: a timeout is a failed evaluation, which `scenarios.md` permits, and a
hypothetical one-damage-per-second build cannot kill a 40,000 health target in
300 s however correct the evaluator is. An operational error stays a distinct
outcome. The boss completion event now says the clock stops at the kill, so a
later window is not part of a completed evaluation.

The four hand-calculated examples are in the suite as
`oracle.worked_examples` and in a protocol table: 2000 damage/s completes at
20.0 s with 0.0 s of untargetable time elapsed, 400 at 115.0 s after one
window, 250 at 190.0 s after both, and 1 times out. They match the reviewer's
arithmetic. `test_the_completion_clock_stops_at_death` recomputes each from the
declared windows rather than reading the stored numbers back, so the examples
cannot drift away from the fixture.

`test_every_limit_leaves_headroom_over_its_own_floor` is removed. It compared
route time plus untargetable time plus preparation against the duration limit
and called the difference headroom, which proved an inequality between
constants and nothing else, and the quantities it summed are not a lower bound
on any evaluation. `test_the_horizon_totals_account_for_the_whole_limit`
replaces it and states what the two figures actually are. No headroom assertion
is made against an unspecified action model.
